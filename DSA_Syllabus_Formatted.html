Course Description

This course develops end-to-end competence in data science and analytics over a 15-week semester. It begins with a structured review of inferential statistics (estimation, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing) and advanced statistics (regression, classification, multivariate methods, and statistical machine learning), then situates these techniques within the principles and lifecycle of data science, including the ethical and responsible use of data.

Students develop hands-on expertise in the Python scientific ecosystem — NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib/Seaborn, SciPy/statsmodels, and scikit-learn — delivered through cloud notebooks, using but not limited to Google Colab, and in data visualization and data storytelling for communicating insights. The course culminates in a research capstone in which student teams design, execute, and write up an original data science and analytics study formatted as a conference paper suitable for submission to a Scopus-indexed conference. Assessment is distributed across the Preliminary, Midterm, and Final examination periods in accordance with the University academic calendar.

Five Course Thrusts(i) Review of inferential statistics · (ii) Review of advanced statistics · (iii) Principles of data science · (iv) Expertise in Python libraries for data science and analytics · (v) Research capstone toward a Scopus-indexed conference paper.
Companion Lecture SeriesThis syllabus is paired with a full set of interactive chapter lecture discussions (Chapters 1–12), each with worked examples, a self-check quiz, a 20-item HOTS test, a 20-item HOTS reinforcement assignment, and a 3-item futures essay — all matched to this outline. Weeks 5, 10, and 15 are examination weeks and carry no new chapter.

Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, the students should be able to:

Knowledge

  1. (CO1) Apply inferential statistical techniques — point and interval estimation, confidence intervals, and parametric and resampling-based hypothesis tests — to draw valid conclusions from sample data.
  2. (CO2) Apply and evaluate advanced statistical methods, including linear and logistic regression, classification, ANOVA, multivariate analysis, clustering, and dimensionality reduction, with attention to model diagnostics, validation, and limitations.
  3. (CO3) Explain the principles, lifecycle, and foundations of data science — from data acquisition and preparation through modeling, evaluation, and deployment.

Skills

  1. (CO4) Demonstrate proficiency in Python and its data science libraries (NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, SciPy/statsmodels, scikit-learn) to acquire, clean, transform, analyze, model, and visualize data.
  2. (CO6) Communicate analytical findings through effective data visualization and data storytelling, and produce a complete research paper that follows the format and quality standards of a Scopus-indexed conference.

Values and Research

  1. (CO5) Design and execute an original data science and analytics research study, from problem formulation and data collection through analysis and interpretation of results.
  2. Uphold the ethical requirements of privacy, consent, fairness, transparency, and data governance in every stage of analytics work, and practice research integrity — honest reporting, proper citation, and similarity-checked writing. Ethics
  3. Embody the RTU core values — Integrity, Innovativeness, Excellence, Nationalism, and Inclusivity — in the responsible use of data for Philippine communities.

Research Capstone: Paper for a Scopus-Indexed Conference

Working in teams of two to four, students conduct an original data science and analytics study on a real dataset and report it as a full conference paper following the template and author guidelines of a specific target conference indexed in Scopus, selected with the Course Professor's approval. The study must demonstrate the complete analytics lifecycle: problem formulation, data acquisition and preparation, exploratory analysis, statistical or machine learning modeling with proper validation, visualization, interpretation, and ethically responsible reporting. Actual submission to the conference is encouraged and mentored but graded on submission-readiness, not on acceptance.

Course Policies

Attendance and Examinations

Attendance and participation follow the RTU Student Handbook. Hands-on Python sessions are conducted within lecture hours using cloud notebooks — Google Colab by default — so a Google account and reliable internet access are expected. The Preliminary, Midterm, and Final Examinations follow the RTU academic calendar; special examinations are governed by University policy. Capstone milestone deadlines are firm because they gate conference-submission timelines.

Academic Integrity and Responsible Use of AI Tools

All submissions pass through similarity checking. Plagiarism, data fabrication, or falsification in any deliverable — including the capstone — results in a failing mark for that requirement and referral under institutional policy. Any use of generative AI in coursework or the capstone must be disclosed and is governed by the institution's academic integrity policy and the target conference's authorship policies.

Data Ethics

Capstone datasets must be legally and ethically obtained; studies involving human-subjects data require clearance under the institution's research ethics protocol before data collection. Data use must remain consistent with the purpose for which the data were collected, with anonymization and secure storage as defaults.

Consultation

Each capstone team is entitled to weekly consultation hours with the Course Professor, Dr. Ryan Manuel D. Guido, from Week 11 onward, in addition to the Week 9–10 proposal consultations. The Teaching Assistant, Instr. Princess B. Tucio, holds coding and Colab-assistance consultation hours weekly throughout the semester and is the first point of contact for practicals, notebook setup, and dataset issues.
